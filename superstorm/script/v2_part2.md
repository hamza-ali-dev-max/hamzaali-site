### Earth's day side and night side · T+14:00

NARR: Where it's night — the Americas, Europe, Africa — the auroras will be spectacular, and the power grids will take the hardest hit.
NARR: Where it's day — Asia, Australia — the storm will arrive at lunchtime, invisible, while people are at work.

### Boulder, USA · 21:00 local · T−00:12 {B1}

NARR: A storm's magnetic field can point north or south. Pointing north, Earth shrugs most of it off. Pointing south, it links up with our own field and pours straight in.
NARR: It points south. And at this speed, the spacecraft buys the planet twelve minutes.

## 4 · T+17 — Impact (19:00–28:00)

### Montreal, Canada · 23:12 local · T+17:00 — scene 1 {M1} {M2}

[Scene 1 (`scene1/`): world map, the aurora crossfade and IMPACT; zoom world → Quebec →
Montreal; Blender top view of the real city; ten districts die north to south in nine seconds;
hospitals come back on generators; 2 s of silence; the Grid Control radio; GNN reporter {M1}
and crowd {M2}; pull back to the world.]

NARR: Hour seventeen. Right on schedule, the storm arrives. Across the northern sky, auroras flare brighter than anyone alive has ever seen.
NARR: And the first place to feel it... is Quebec.
NARR: Its power grid runs across ancient bedrock that can't absorb the surge. In 1989, a storm a fraction of this size took this grid down in ninety seconds. Tonight... it takes nine.
GRID: Grid Control to all stations... we've lost the northern lines. Multiple transformer trips... Montreal is down. I repeat, we are losing the network... we are losing the
GNN: I'm in downtown Montreal, where just a minute ago every light in the city went out at once. People are pouring into the streets... and above us, you can see it, the whole sky is green. We're hearing the entire province may be without power.
NARR: Nine million people. In the dark. And the storm is only getting started.

### New York, USA · 23:16 local · T+17:04 {N2}

[Map south along the coast, zoom into New York (aurora map). {N2} hands over into the real
Manhattan at night, then block after block goes dark. Caption: NORTHEAST GRID: COLLAPSE.
Counter: 9 M → 31 M.]

NARR: When Quebec's grid drops, its neighbours lose thousands of megawatts of imports in an instant. Frequencies sag. Protection relays do exactly what they're built to do. They let go.
NARR: Four minutes later, the American Northeast. Manhattan goes dark from the top down — the tallest towers first, their elevators stopping between floors.

### London, United Kingdom · 04:21 local · T+17:09 {X1} {L2}

[Map across the Atlantic, zoom into London (aurora map). {X1} hands over into the real city:
the Thames, Tower Bridge, a red aurora. PiP {L2}: the reporter on the dark street.

LONDON: The lights went out at about twenty past four. What you can hear is... nothing. No traffic. Just people outside, looking up.
NARR: It's four in the morning in Britain. Most people sleep through the moment it happens. They wake up to silent fridges, dead phones, and a sky still glowing red.

### Stockholm, Sweden · 05:24 local · T+17:12 {S1}

[Zoom into Stockholm (aurora map). {S1} hands over into the real city under a green aurora,
trams moving, then the lights go out. Caption: RAIL SIGNALS: ALL RED. Counter: 58 M → 94 M.]

VOX_SE: Det är grönt överallt... och sen släcktes allt.
*(It's green everywhere... and then everything went out.)*
NARR: Scandinavia sits on the same kind of ancient rock as Quebec. The currents have nowhere to go but into the power lines — and into the railway signals, which fail safe. Every signal in the country turns red.

### Dunedin, New Zealand · 17:30 local · T+17:18

[Map across to the South Pacific, zoom into Dunedin (day map). Caption: TRANSFORMER FAILURE.
Sound: a deep electrical hum rising, a bang, silence.]

NARR: Not every failure happens in the dark. In daylight, in New Zealand, a transformer overheats and dies.
NARR: It happened here once before, in 2001, in a storm that most people never noticed. Engineers in Dunedin have been waiting for this one for twenty-five years.

### Tokyo, Japan · 13:12 local · T+17:00

[Zoom into Tokyo (day). Radio panel: KANTO NEWS 24. Map: Hokkaido flushes red, Kanto stays gold.
Caption: HOKKAIDO: BLACKOUT · KANTO: HOLDING.]

TOKYO: 北海道と東北の一部で停電が発生しています。関東の電力網は今のところ持ちこたえています。
*(Blackouts are being reported in parts of Hokkaido and Tohoku. The Kanto grid is holding for now.)*
NARR: Japan's grid bends but mostly holds. Shorter lines, different rock, and a lot of luck.
NARR: Which makes the next hours even stranger. Half the planet is watching the other half go dark — live, on the internet.

### Singapore · 12:40 local · T+17:28

[Zoom into Singapore (day). Caption: GPS ERROR: 60 M. Graphic: container-crane icons freeze.]

NARR: In Singapore, the busiest port in the world, the automated cranes stop and wait for GPS to make sense again.
NARR: Satellite navigation needs the upper atmosphere to be calm. Tonight, it's boiling. Positions drift by sixty metres, and for some receivers, they simply stop.

### Orbit · T+17:48

[Map: satellite dots flicker; forty of them turn red and fall out of their orbit band.
Caption: CONTACT LOST: 214 SATELLITES.]

NARR: The storm heats the upper atmosphere, and it swells. For satellites in low orbit, it's like driving into a wall of air.
NARR: Forty of them never make it back up. It happened to a single company's fleet in 2022, in a storm a hundred times weaker than this one.

## 5 · The First Night (28:00–36:00)

### New York, USA · 00:10 local · T+18:00 {N3}

[Zoom into Manhattan (blackout map). Full screen {N3}: the line of people in the tunnel.
Caption: 480 TRAINS STOPPED.]

VOX_NY: Stay together, stay together, watch the third rail — it's dead, it's dead, just walk.
NARR: When the power dropped, four hundred and eighty subway trains stopped wherever they were. Most of them in tunnels.
NARR: It takes the city until three in the morning to walk everyone out.

### Chicago, USA · 23:30 local · T+18:18

[Zoom into Chicago (blackout map). Radio panel: CITY DISPATCH. Caption: 911: 6,000 CALLS
WAITING. Graphic: traffic-light icons across the city switch off.]

DISPATCH: All units, we are holding six thousand calls. Priority one only. Priority one only.
NARR: Every traffic light in Chicago is dark. Every elevator alarm is ringing. And the phone network is running on batteries that were designed to last a few hours.

### The Atlantic · T+18:48

[Map: undersea cables appear as thin cyan lines across the oceans. One by one, the North
Atlantic lines blink amber, then red. Caption: TRANSATLANTIC CAPACITY: −78 %.]

NARR: Undersea cables are powered from the shore, through repeaters spaced along the sea floor. The storm pushes current into them too.
NARR: One by one, the lines across the Atlantic go quiet. The internet doesn't go down. It splits — into pieces that can't talk to each other.

### Paris, France · 07:30 local · T+19:18 {P1}

[Zoom into Paris (blackout map, dawn). Full screen {P1}: the bridge full of walking commuters.
Caption: MÉTRO : FERMÉ.]

VOX_FR: Pas de métro, pas de lumière, pas de réseau. On marche.
*(No metro, no lights, no signal. We walk.)*
NARR: France keeps most of its power — its grid is dense and strong. But it imports from its neighbours, and the neighbours are dark. By morning, the Paris Métro is shut, and a million people walk to work.

### São Paulo, Brazil · 02:00 local · T+18:48

[Zoom into São Paulo (half the map lights out). Caption: PARTIAL BLACKOUT. Sound: helicopters.]

VOX_BR: Metade da cidade apagou. A outra metade tá rezando.
*(Half the city went dark. The other half is praying.)*
NARR: Far from the magnetic poles, South America should be safe. But the storm is so large that the auroras reach Brazil, and so do the currents.

### Shanghai, China · 13:30 local · T+18:18

[Zoom into Shanghai (day). Map: northern China's grid flushes red, the coast stays gold.
Caption: NORTHERN GRID: 38 % LOST.]

NARR: China's grid bends. Long lines in the north trip; the coast holds. Factories go quiet across three provinces.
NARR: Somewhere in those factories are the parts the rest of the world will need next month.

### Montreal, Canada · 04:00 local · T+21:48

[Top view (Blender, no footage): the dark city, a few hospitals lit. Radio panel: CITY
EMERGENCY — WARMING CENTRES. Caption: −14 °C · WARMING CENTRES: 40.]

NARR: Four in the morning in Montreal, minus fourteen. The first warming centres fill up.
NARR: A house without heat loses about a degree an hour. By tomorrow night, a lot of homes will be colder inside than a fridge.

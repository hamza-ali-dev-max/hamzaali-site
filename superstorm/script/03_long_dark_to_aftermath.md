# Script — Part 3: The Long Dark → Breakdown → Aftermath (33:00–62:00)

## Chapter 6 — Day 2–3: The Long Dark (33:00–42:00)

**33:00 | DAY 2 card (HUD style). Map, morning: the blackout regions now drawn as dark patches with red borders; HUD: PEOPLE WITHOUT POWER 212,000,000 · ESTIMATED DEATHS 41.**

**NARR**:
> Day two. The storm is still raging — it will keep the grid unstable for another thirty hours.
> Engineers try to restart Quebec's network, and discover what the ground currents did overnight.

**33:40 | Radio panel: GRID CONTROL — CH 2.**

**GRID** (exhausted):
> Substation report from the north corridor: three transformers with winding damage. Two with confirmed insulation failure. We're not re-energising those. Not today, not this week.

**NARR**:
> Some transformers can be restarted. Some are cooked from the inside. Remember: a replacement takes one to two years. And every grid on Earth is now trying to order one at the same time.

**34:40 | Montreal day aerial of a silent city, no traffic, smoke from chimneys.** `[FULL: winter city daylight aerial, empty roads]`

**NARR**:
> Then the slow failures begin — the ones nobody sees on the news.
> Water. Montreal's water is pumped. Without power, pressure drops in the high-rise floors first, then whole neighbourhoods.
> Gas stations have fuel underground — and no power to pump it. Cash registers are dead. The ATMs are dead. Suddenly, the only money that works is paper, and nobody has any.

**35:50 | `[PiP ×3 with arrows]` queue at a gas station with hand-written sign · line for water at a fire hydrant · people burning furniture in a fireplace.**

**VOX** (FR, tired): *On a de l'eau pour deux jours. Après ça, je sais pas.* *(We've got water for two days. After that, I don't know.)*

**36:40 | ESTIMATED DEATHS ticks faster: 41 → 187.**

**NARR**:
> The deaths start to follow a pattern. Carbon monoxide — people running generators and barbecues indoors to stay warm. House fires from candles. Hypothermia in older homes. Patients who depend on machines: dialysis, ventilators, oxygen.
> None of it is spectacular. That's what makes it so deadly.

**37:40 | Hospital: day 2 evening. `[PiP: generator yard, diesel tank gauge near empty]`. Radio: ST-LAURENT GENERAL.**

**FACILITIES** (radio, tense):
> Our resupply truck didn't come. Supplier says his depot pumps have no power either. We're at twenty-two hours of diesel. Start moving patients who can be moved.

**NARR**:
> Remember the seventy-two hours? The plan assumed the fuel trucks would keep coming. The fuel trucks assumed the pumps would keep working. The pumps assumed the grid.

**38:50 | Europe: London & Stockholm day 2. `[PiP: London supermarket, empty shelves, security guard]` · `[PiP: Swedish town, people collecting firewood]`. Headline cards: "Britain declares national emergency" · "Nordic grids: weeks, not days".**

**39:50 | First looting. `[PiP: night street, shop window broken, silhouettes, flashlights]` arrow → Montreal; a second → New Jersey.**

**NARR**:
> On the second night, it starts. Not everywhere, and not by most people — but it only takes a few.
> A pharmacy in the east end. Electronics stores in New Jersey. A generator yard in Philadelphia, emptied in forty minutes.

**40:50 | Day 3 card. HUD: ESTIMATED DEATHS 612.**

**NARR**:
> By day three, the storm itself is over. The auroras fade.
> The blackout isn't.

## Chapter 7 — Week 1–2: Breakdown (42:00–52:00)

**42:00 | WEEK 1 card. World map: dark regions; lit regions (Japan, Australia, most of South America, Africa, southern Europe) marked as "GRID STABLE". Arrows of fuel tankers crossing oceans toward dark regions.**

**NARR**:
> One week in. Around four hundred and eighty million people are without reliable power — spread across North America, Northern Europe, and pockets of Asia.
> Governments now face the question that decides everything: who gets electricity first?

**43:00 | Montreal: `[PiP: military trucks at a hospital, soldiers unloading fuel]`. HUD: CURFEW 20:00.**

**NARR**:
> Soldiers guard fuel convoys. Hospitals first, then water plants, then emergency shelters. Curfews at eight p.m.
> Generators become the most valuable object in the city. People are killed over them.

**44:10 | Headline cards, rapid: "Generator thefts surge" · "Black market diesel hits $30 a litre" · "Shelters at capacity".**

**44:40 | THE WAR OVER TRANSFORMERS. Map: the handful of transformer factories marked with amber icons (fictional labels: "Plant A — Asia", "Plant B — Europe"...). Order counters stack up: 1,900 units requested; world capacity: ~300/year.**

**NARR**:
> Then comes the part nobody planned for. Almost every damaged grid needs the same thing: large power transformers. The world can build a few hundred a year.
> Nineteen hundred are ordered in ten days.

**45:40 | Map: countries with factories turn a hard amber: EXPORT BAN. Ships with transformer icons stop mid-ocean.**

**NARR**:
> The countries that make them do the rational thing. They ban exports — they keep them for themselves.
> The countries that need them do the rational thing too. They start making threats.

**46:30 | Map: two naval icons converge near a cargo ship symbol in a strait. `[PiP: warships at sea, grey sky, no markings]`. HUD: NAVAL STANDOFF.**

**GNN ANCHOR** `[PiP]`:
> A cargo ship carrying eleven large transformers has been stopped at sea tonight, as two navies argue over who they belong to. Diplomats are calling it the most dangerous standoff in decades.

**NARR**:
> It's not a world war. It's worse in a way — it's a hundred small conflicts over the same scarce things. Transformers. Diesel. Copper. Grid-scale batteries. Even the specialised steel the transformers are built from.

**47:50 | Local conflict: `[PiP: crowd confrontation outside a fuel depot, police line, shouting, no weapons shown]`.**

**RADIO** (police channel, FR):
> *Toutes les unités au dépôt de la rue Notre-Dame, foule hostile, deux cents personnes…* *(All units to the Notre-Dame Street depot, hostile crowd, two hundred people…)*

**48:40 | WEEK 2 card. HUD: ESTIMATED DEATHS 3,480 (climbing). Death causes panel (small bar chart, HUD style): COLD · CARBON MONOXIDE · MEDICAL DEVICES · HOSPITAL FAILURES · VIOLENCE · ACCIDENTS.**

**NARR**:
> Two weeks in, the numbers stop feeling like numbers.
> Cold, and carbon monoxide, and the loss of medical care kill far more people than any riot. Most of them die quietly, at home, in the dark — and many aren't found for days.

**50:00 | Tokyo & Sydney lit: `[PiP: Tokyo reporter in a bright studio]`.**

**TOKYO** (JA):
> 日本の工場は24時間体制で変圧器を製造していますが、注文は数年分に達しています。
> *(Japanese factories are building transformers around the clock, but the orders add up to several years' worth.)*

**51:00 | Hook: map of North America, a few scattered points of gold light return, then flicker off.**

**NARR**:
> And in Quebec, engineers are about to try the one thing that could bring the lights back in weeks instead of years.

## Chapter 8 — Month 1–6: Aftermath (52:00–60:00)

**52:00 | MONTH 1 card. Radio panel: GRID CONTROL — CH 1.**

**GRID** (steady again):
> Grid Control to all stations. We're energising the Manicouagan corridor with the three repaired units and two spares from storage. Stand by… closing breakers now.

**52:40 | Map zoom to Quebec: gold lines light up from the north down to Montreal. Districts relight one by one (the reverse of the blackout wave), satisfying sound design (rising hum). HUD PEOPLE WITHOUT POWER in Quebec: 9,000,000 → 5,400,000.**

**NARR**:
> They cannibalise. Spare transformers from warehouses. Units pulled from less important substations. Rewired, derated, run at half their rating.
> On day thirty-one, Montreal's lights start coming back. Neighbourhood by neighbourhood.

**53:50 | `[PiP: apartment window, a family cheers as the lights come on]` · `[PiP: street, streetlights flicker on, people clapping]`.**

**VOX** (FR, emotional): *La lumière! La lumière est revenue!* *(The light! The light is back!)*

**54:40 | World map, months 2–6 time-lapse: dark regions shrink; some stay dark longer (rural areas, parts of Northern Europe).**

**NARR**:
> Recovery isn't a moment. It's a map that slowly fills back in.
> Big cities come first. Rural lines last — some for six months.

**55:40 | FINAL TALLY (HUD full screen): PEOPLE WHO LOST POWER 612,000,000 · LONGEST OUTAGE 7 MONTHS · ESTIMATED DEATHS 18,900 (range 9,000–35,000) · ECONOMIC DAMAGE $2.7 TRILLION.**

**NARR**:
> In this simulation, six hundred million people lost power. Around nineteen thousand died — most of them not in the first night, but in the weeks after.
> The damage: nearly three trillion dollars. And the thing that caused it lasted three days.

**57:00 | The Sun, calm, turning. `[FULL: sun close-up, quiet]`**

**NARR**:
> Here's the part that isn't a simulation.
> Storms like this aren't rare on the Sun's timescale. The last one this size hit Earth in 1859. In 2012, one just as big crossed Earth's orbit — and missed us by about nine days.

**58:10 | Map: a real-world readiness list in HUD style (generic, no brands): SPARE TRANSFORMER RESERVES · GROUND-CURRENT BLOCKERS · SATELLITE EARLY WARNING · HOSPITAL FUEL RULES.**

**NARR**:
> We know how to make grids tougher: blockers for ground currents, spare transformers stored in advance, better warning satellites, hospitals that plan for weeks instead of days.
> Some of that is already happening. Most of it is waiting for the storm to make it urgent.

**59:30 | Back to Montreal at dawn, lights on. Radio dial static → the jingle.**

**ANIM** (FR, softer):
> Bonjour Montréal. Il est six heures. Moins quatre degrés ce matin… et ciel clair.
> *(Good morning Montreal. It's six o'clock. Minus four this morning… and clear skies.)*

**NARR**:
> The next one isn't a question of if.
> Just when.
